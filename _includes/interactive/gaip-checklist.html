01 Human-centric by design Not Started
AI supports, not replaces, human decision-making. Final accountability for regulatory decisions and patient safety remains with qualified human experts.
02 Risk-based approach Not Started
Governance proportional to risk. High-risk applications impacting patient safety or submission integrity require more rigorous validation and oversight than low-risk uses.
03 Adherence to standards Not Started
AI must comply with existing GxP, ICH, and data privacy standards. AI does not exempt any process from requirements that apply to non-AI approaches.
04 Clear context of use Not Started
Document the AI tool's intended purpose, input data, scope of decisions, and limitations. Context must be specific enough for a regulator to assess what the AI does and does not do.
05 Multidisciplinary expertise Not Started
Involve domain experts (clinical, regulatory, safety), data scientists, QA, and IT. No single group should own AI governance alone.
06 Data governance and documentation Not Started
Rigorous data governance: quality assessment, provenance tracking, version control, and audit trails. All training, validation, and test data must be traceable and reproducible.
07 Model design and development practices Not Started
Use rigorous engineering: clear problem definition, reproducible training pipelines, systematic tuning, and testing against predefined acceptance criteria.
08 Risk-based performance assessment Not Started
Assess performance with risk-appropriate metrics: accuracy, precision, recall, robustness, fairness, and drift monitoring. Predefine and document performance thresholds.
09 Life cycle management Not Started
Manage AI across the full lifecycle: development, validation, deployment, monitoring, maintenance, and retirement. Each phase needs documented processes, version control, and impact assessments.
10 Clear, essential information Not Started
Clearly disclose where and how AI was used in submissions. Document the tool's role, limitations, validation status, and human oversight so regulators can assess output reliability.
